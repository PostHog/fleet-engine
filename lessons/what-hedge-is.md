## Two boxes are starting for you

While you watch, your setup agent starts two boxes. A hub and a node, in the cloud. By the end, you know what each one does. And you know where you step in.

## Your Mac only shows the work

Your Mac runs only the T3 Code app. The agents run on Linux boxes in the cloud. So they keep working when you close the lid. And you can follow them from your phone.

## Nodes do the work

Every thread you start runs on a node. Each one gets its own copy of the repository. So four threads can work at once. They never get in each other's way. Need more at once? You add a node.

## The hub holds the keys

The hub is the box in the middle. It keeps your sign-ins for Slack and other tools. It links the nodes and watches their disks. With own-account balancing on, it also holds your Claude subscriptions. They sit in one proxy. It spends the one whose weekly quota resets first.

## The hub listens while you are away

The hub also runs small services. With the Slack pack, a reaction starts a thread. React to a Slack message with the T3 Code emoji. With recovery on, a usage limit only pauses a thread. It continues once the quota is back. And each night, every box updates itself.

## Threads come to you

You do not watch threads. A thread comes to you when it finishes. Or when it needs you. It lands in your T3 Code inbox. On the Mac, or on the phone. A decision comes as a question card. The card has a recommended answer. So most answers take one tap.

## Answer, snooze, settle

Every thread ends with one next step for you. Waiting on someone else? It snoozes until there is news. When the work is done, you settle it. Then it leaves the inbox. You can also schedule a thread. Such as a check the morning after a deploy.

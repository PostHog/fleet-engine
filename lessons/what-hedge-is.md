## Two boxes are starting for you

While you watch this video, your setup agent starts two boxes in the cloud: a hub and a node. By the end, you know what each one does, and where you step in.

## Your Mac only shows the work

Your Mac runs only the T3 Code app. The agents run on Linux boxes in the cloud, so they keep working when you close the lid, and you can follow them from your phone.

## Nodes do the work

Every thread you start runs on a node, in its own copy of the repository, so four threads can work at once without getting in each other's way. When you need more at once, you add a node.

## The hub holds the keys

The hub is the box in the middle. It keeps the sign-ins for Slack and your other tools, links the nodes, and watches their disks. With own-account balancing on, it also holds your Claude subscriptions in one proxy, and spends the one whose weekly quota resets first.

## The hub listens while you are away

The hub also runs small services. With the Slack pack, a Slack message you react to with the T3 Code emoji starts a thread. With recovery on, a thread that stops at a usage limit continues once the quota is back. And each night, every box updates itself.

## Threads come to you

You do not watch threads. A thread that finishes, or needs you, comes to your T3 Code inbox, on the Mac or the phone. A decision comes as a question card with a recommended answer, so most answers take one tap.

## Answer, snooze, settle

Every thread ends with one next step for you. When it waits on someone else, it snoozes until there is news. When the work is done, you settle it, and it leaves the inbox. You can also schedule a thread, such as a check the morning after a deploy.
